# Which Foods Naturally Boost Nitric Oxide Production?

The best natural source of nitric oxide is nitrate-rich vegetables, with beetroot (beets) and leafy greens leading the list.

Strictly speaking, no food contains nitric oxide.

Your body makes it, and vegetables supply nitrate that your body can convert into it.

Medical News Today describes nitrate from vegetables being converted into nitric oxide, which relaxes blood vessels and may help lower blood pressure.

This guide explains what nitric oxide is, what it does, [which foods and drinks boost nitric oxide production](https://bakrus-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/what-are-the-best-natural-sources-of-nitric-oxide), what the evidence says about herbs, ashwagandha and supplements, and where the marketing runs ahead of the science.

It is general information, not medical advice.

## What Is Nitric Oxide and What Does It Do?

If you ask what nitric oxide is or what nitric oxide does, here is the plain version. Nitric oxide is a gas molecule your body makes.

In the circulation, it helps relax and widen blood vessels.

Medical News Today's[ article on nitrate-rich vegetables and blood pressure](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/259113/), published April 16, 2013 by Sarah Glynn, explains that nitrate from vegetables is converted into nitric oxide, which relaxes blood vessels and may help lower blood pressure.

The article reports a small study by Queen Mary University of London, published in Hypertension and funded by the British Heart Foundation.

In 15 adults with high blood pressure, 250 mL of beetroot juice (about 0.2 g of nitrate) lowered systolic pressure by about 10 points over 24 hours compared with a low-nitrate drink.

The lead author said it is uncertain whether the effect lasts long term, and the study was small.

These are the main nitric oxide benefits that have some research behind them. Claims about energy, stamina or sexual performance go further than the studies I read.

## What Does Nitric Oxide Do for Men?

What does nitric oxide do for men? The same thing it does for everyone: it helps blood vessels relax, which matters for blood flow.

Erectile function depends on blood flow, so nitric oxide is part of the story, but erectile dysfunction has many causes, including stress, hormones, heart health and medicines.

A supplement article from Hearst (via Yahoo, with no date or byline shown) says evidence for arginine and citrulline in people without erectile dysfunction is limited.

If you have symptoms, see a doctor rather than relying on a supplement.

## Four Drinks and Beetroot Juice

The phrase four drinks that will directly boost nitric oxide oversells the science, because no drink delivers nitric oxide directly.

What I can support is beetroot juice.

A 2018 systematic review in Biomolecules,[ dietary nitrate from beetroot juice for hypertension](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6316347), analyzed 11 randomized trials with 310 participants in total and found that beetroot juice appeared to lower blood pressure, with a larger effect on systolic pressure. The trials were small and varied, the effect differed by age and other factors, trials using nitrate-depleted juice sometimes found no effect, and the doses of 300 to 500 mg nitrate exceeded the WHO acceptable daily intake.

Two authors have ties to supplement companies.

So a realistic list of four drinks to try is:

1. Beetroot juice (the only one with trial support here)
2. A green smoothie or vegetable juice built on leafy greens
3. A homemade juice with beets and a little lemon
4. Water and unsweetened tea to support hydration

Drinks 2 to 4 are my practical suggestions based on the nitrate-rich vegetables above, not drinks I could verify.

Pomegranate and watermelon juice are often named online, but I did not verify them for this article.

Beetroot juice can turn urine pink and may not suit everyone, so check with a doctor if you take blood pressure medicine.

## Herbs, Ashwagandha and Nitric Oxide

For nitric oxide herbs and herbs that increase nitric oxide, I found no reliable human evidence in this session.

On ashwagandha nitric oxide, a Ubie Doctor's Note article on[ ashwagandha and erectile dysfunction](https://ubiehealth.com/doctors-note/ashwagandha-erectile-dysfunction-evidence-stress-711e3), published January 13, 2026 and reviewed for accuracy June 23, 2026 by Yoshinori Abe, MD, says ashwagandha may improve nitric oxide signaling, but the only support cited is animal studies and no human nitric oxide data.

It describes two small, short trials of up to 64 participants and about 3 months that did not measure erectile function directly, and calls for larger placebo-controlled trials.

It also lists mild side effects such as stomach upset, headache and drowsiness, and advises caution in pregnancy, with autoimmune conditions, sedatives or thyroid medicines, and with prescription erectile dysfunction drugs.

So I would not rely on ashwagandha as a nitric oxide strategy.

## Nitric Oxide Supplements and Boosters

A nitric oxide supplement usually doesn't contain nitric oxide.

The Hearst article says most use precursors such as L-arginine and L-citrulline.

It says evidence is mixed for exercise performance, inconclusive for blood pressure and heart health, and that the body usually makes enough on its own.

Reported side effects include vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness and headaches, supplements aren't regulated like medicines, and some products contain caffeine.

People with high blood pressure, heart problems, kidney disease or low blood pressure should ask a doctor first.

The article is undated and has no byline, so treat it as a general summary.

## How to Increase Nitric Oxide Naturally

For how to increase nitric oxide naturally, build a routine around food:

* Eat leafy greens, lettuce, cabbage, fennel and beets regularly.
* Include beans, nuts, seeds, fish and dairy for protein building blocks.
* Consider beetroot juice, in moderation, if your doctor agrees.
* Keep up other habits for blood pressure, such as less salt and a healthy weight, which Medical News Today mentions alongside vegetables.

I did not verify claims that exercise, sunlight or mouthwash habits change nitric oxide, so I left them out.

A doctor is the right person to ask about blood pressure and erectile symptoms.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor if you have high blood pressure, chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, persistent erectile problems or take heart or blood pressure medicine and want to try beetroot juice or a supplement.

Seek urgent care for chest pain or fainting.
